Just been named an executor? Your first week, step by step (England and Wales)

By Hope, who works in probate in the UK · Updated 11 November 2026

If you've just been asked to deal with someone's estate, it can feel overwhelming. Most of what matters in the first week is simple and practical. This is the order many families find helpful.

The first days

  • Collect the medical certificate of cause of death from the doctor or hospital.
  • Register the death. In England and Wales this should usually be done within five days.
  • Order several certified copies of the death certificate when you register. Banks and pension providers often want to see an original copy.
  • Use Tell Us Once, usually offered when you register, to notify many government departments in one go.
  • Find the original Will and keep it safe.

Look after the home and belongings

  • Secure the property, collect keys and make valuables safe.
  • Tell the home insurer straight away if the property will be empty. Many policies limit cover once a home has been unoccupied for a set period.
  • Make sure any pets or animals are cared for.
  • Redirect the post.

Start a record

  • Write down every organisation you contact, the date, who you spoke to and any reference number. It saves repeating yourself and helps if anything is queried later.
  • Keep every receipt for anything you pay. Reasonable expenses of acting as executor can usually be repaid from the estate.

Be kind to yourself

  • Nobody gets this done in a week. Much of the estate work (valuing, probate, paying debts and sharing the estate) happens over the following months.
  • Executors can ask a solicitor or probate firm to help with some or all of the work, especially where there's property, inheritance tax or disagreement in the family.

Questions

How long do I have to register a death?

In England and Wales, a death should usually be registered within five days. Scotland allows eight days.

What is Tell Us Once?

A free government service that lets you report a death to many government departments at once, such as HMRC, the DWP, the DVLA and the local council. It doesn't cover banks, energy companies or subscriptions.

Do I have to deal with the estate myself?

No. Executors can use a professional for some or all of the work.

This guide is general information for England and Wales. It is not legal, financial or veterinary advice. Rules can change, so check GOV.UK or speak to a qualified professional about your situation.
