How to keep estate accounts as an executor (UK)

By Hope, who works in probate in the UK · Updated 11 November 2026

Beneficiaries are entitled to see how an estate has been dealt with. Clear accounts are also the best way to avoid misunderstandings in a family. Here is what to record, and when.

What the estate owned and owed at the date of death

  • Ask each bank for a letter showing the balance on the date of death.
  • Get valuations for property and significant possessions, and write down where each figure came from.
  • List debts and bills owed at the date of death, and the funeral costs.

Money in and money out

  • Log every payment into the estate as it happens: closed accounts, refunds, sale proceeds, interest.
  • Log every payment out: debts, bills, fees and executor expenses, with receipts.
  • Where possible, open a separate executor or estate bank account. Every payment then has its own clear trail.

Before sharing anything out

  • Be cautious about paying beneficiaries before every debt and tax bill is known. Executors can be personally responsible if money runs out.
  • Inheritance tax, where it's due, generally has to be paid by the end of the sixth month after the death.
  • Many executors wait until six months after the grant before paying out the whole estate, because certain family members and dependants can bring a claim within that period.

The final account

  • Start with the assets at death, take off debts, funeral costs and any inheritance tax, add income received and take off expenses, then specific gifts. What's left is the residue to share.
  • Show how the residue was shared, and ask each beneficiary to sign a short receipt.

Questions

Do executors have to keep accounts?

Executors have a duty to administer the estate properly and to account to beneficiaries. Keeping clear records from the start makes that far easier.

Can I use a spreadsheet?

Yes. Many executors use a spreadsheet so the totals add up automatically.

Does an estate account work out inheritance tax?

No. Inheritance tax depends on the estate and the rules that apply. Use HMRC's guidance or professional advice.
