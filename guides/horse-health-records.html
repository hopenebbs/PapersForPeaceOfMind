Horse health records: what every UK owner should keep

By Hope, who works in probate in the UK · Updated 11 November 2026

Every horse owner has a drawer of vet invoices and a rough memory of when the farrier last came. Keeping a simple record helps your horse, your vet and anyone who looks after them while you're away.

The essentials

  • Passport number or UELN, microchip number, and where the passport is kept. The passport should travel with the horse.
  • Insurer, policy and renewal date.
  • Your horse's team: vet, out-of-hours vet, farrier, dentist, physio and saddle fitter.

Dates to track

  • Flu and tetanus vaccinations. If you compete, check your governing body's rules, as some require flu boosters every six months, recorded in the passport.
  • Faecal egg counts, tapeworm tests and any wormer given. Many vets now recommend testing first and worming only when needed.
  • Farrier and dentist visits, and saddle fit checks.
  • Vet visits, what was found and what was done, and whether you claimed on insurance.

What's normal for them

  • Knowing your horse's normal temperature, pulse and breathing rate at rest helps you spot when something's wrong.
  • Weight and body condition over time.
  • Feed, turnout, rugging and any quirks, so anyone could step in tomorrow.

Questions

How often should horses be vaccinated?

Follow your vet's advice. If you compete, check your governing body's rules, as some require more frequent flu boosters.

Do I have to microchip my horse?

In England, all horses, ponies and donkeys must be microchipped.

Should I worm on a fixed schedule?

Many vets now recommend faecal egg counts and testing first, then worming only when needed. Ask your vet for a plan.

This guide is general information for England and Wales. It is not legal, financial or veterinary advice. Rules can change, so check GOV.UK or speak to a qualified professional about your situation.
